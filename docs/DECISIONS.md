## 2026-11-15: Common window e = −3 … 0 for the cross-state pooling

- **Decision:** (user) New spec `common_window` in steps 5, 6 and 8: the main specification (screens, controls, weights, clustering) on e = −3 … 0 in every state, with its own balance and senior-share screens over those classes (`in_window_common`, `f_balanced_common`, `f_senior_share_ok_common`). `12_aggregation.R` pools it as the main input. Deneault's window (`main`) stays the state headline (Louisiana replication) and is pooled as a window sensitivity check (main prior only). `13_baggr_check.R` and `14_pooling_report.R` follow the main input. The rule for which specs drop FRL in NO_FRL_STATES is now one helper, `06_first_stage.spec_controls`, used by steps 6 and 8.
- **Alternatives considered:** e = −4 … 0 with Louisiana (FSA from 2015) and Texas enrollment (THECB campus files from fall 2019) starting at e = −3; pooling the headline estimates (windows differ across states).
- **Reason:** Each state's β compares post classes with the mean of its pre-window classes, which include the dose classes (e = −3, −2) and their mechanical dip. So the baseline must be the same in event time everywhere. Event-study approximations showed that dropping e = −4 raises β by 0.01–0.04 in the first stage (9 of 10 states) and by 0.01–0.03 in enrollment, so states with and without e = −4 were not comparable. Every state can supply e = −3 … 0, and e = 0 is the design's common post class.
- **Result:** First stage, common vs. Deneault window: AL 0.148/0.142, CA 0.196/0.112, IL 0.216/0.093, IN 0.215/0.211, LA 0.356/0.377, NE 0.111/0.092, NH 0.254/0.220, NJ 0.204/0.182, NY 0.087/0.076, OK 0.246/0.206, TX 0.191/0.168. Enrollment: AL 0.045/0.051, CA 0.065/0.044, IL 0.017/−0.013, IN 0.045/0.035, LA 0.093/0.060, NE 0.040/0.022, NH 0.112/0.094, TX 0.092/0.073. School counts rise slightly, since balance is needed over 4 classes (e.g. TX first stage 1,318 → 1,396). Pooled (main prior, no covariate): FAFSA completion mu 0.197 [0.145, 0.252], sigma_beta 0.074 [0.038, 0.134] (Deneault window: 0.166, 0.089); enrollment mu 0.062 [0.031, 0.092], sigma_beta 0.027 [0.002, 0.070] (0.044, 0.030). baggr matches all 22 parameters.
- **Caveat:** two of the common window's three pre classes are dose classes, so the mechanical dip likely raises every state's β and mu. The common window makes this bias equal across states, not zero; the fix belongs to the pre-trend plan (dose from classes outside the window).
- **Affects:** `05_build_analysis_panel.py` (`WINDOWS`, `window_classes(df, window)`), `06_first_stage.py` (`COMMON_SCREENS`, `SPECS`, `FRL_SPECS`, `spec_panel`, `spec_controls`), `08_reduced_form.py`, `12–14`, `first_stage_by_state.csv`, `reduced_form_by_state.csv`, pooling outputs (`input_spec`), codebook, tests.

## 2026-11-15: Pooling priors set to the weakly informative spec; Meager pooling metrics

- **Decision:** (user spec) The main priors in `12_aggregation.R` are weakly informative on the 0–1 dose scale: mu ~ N(0, 1), delta ~ N(0, 0.5²), sigma_beta ~ Half-Normal(0, 0.5), with all scales halved for enrollment (N(0, 0.5²), N(0, 0.25²), Half-Normal(0, 0.25)). The posterior is still exact (normal priors on mu and delta are conjugate). Sensitivity: `flat_uniform` (the previous main: flat mu, delta, uniform(0, 1) sigma_beta) and `half_cauchy_05` (main mu, delta priors, half-Cauchy(0, 0.05) sigma_beta). The previous `half_cauchy_50` run is dropped. The pooling metric is now Meager's: `omega_k` = se_k² ÷ (sigma_beta² + se_k²) per state (her eq. 6), its average `omega_bar`, both at the posterior mean of sigma_beta², plus Gelman & Pardoe's `lambda_gp` (her eq. 8). These replace `pooling_factor`, which was mean se² ÷ (mean se² + E[sigma_beta²]). `13_baggr_check.R` now uses the same main priors (baggr's `normal` on the hyper-SD is half-normal), so the cross-check compares identical models.
- **Reason:** The spec was not recorded in the repo when the pooling was first written, so textbook defaults were used.
- **Result (main prior, no covariate):** FAFSA completion mu 0.166 [0.105, 0.228], sigma_beta 0.089 [0.047, 0.159], omega_bar 0.17, lambda_gp 0.23. College enrollment mu 0.044 [0.012, 0.075], sigma_beta 0.029 [0.003, 0.073], omega_bar 0.42, lambda_gp 0.66. These are within simulation noise of the flat/uniform results: the data dominate these priors. baggr matches all 22 parameters (max R-hat 1.00).
- **Affects:** `code/12_aggregation.R`, `code/13_baggr_check.R`, `pooling_hyper.csv` (`prior` labels), `pooling_states.csv` (`omega_k`, `omega_bar`, `lambda_gp` replace `pooling_factor`), `pooling_baggr_check.csv`, codebook, `tests/test_pipeline.py` (`TestAggregation`: prior label `uniform` → `main`; new `test_pooling_metrics`).
- **Related issue:**

## 2026-11-13: Pool state estimates with an exact normal hierarchical model in base R

- **Decision:** `code/12_aggregation.R` pools each state's `main` first-stage and reduced-form `beta` (SE treated as known) with the Meager (2019) / Rubin (1981) normal hierarchical model. The posterior is computed exactly (tau on a 5,000-point grid over (0, 1], then mu, gamma, theta from their normal conditionals; BDA3 ch. 5), not by MCMC. Main prior: flat on mu, uniform on tau; half-Cauchy(0.05) and half-Cauchy(0.5) on tau as prior sensitivity. The state covariate is `no_diploma` (`graduation_consequence == none`: CA, NY, PA), since it separates the pooled policy types into two groups and gives PA a value. CT and PA get posterior predictive effects as an out-of-sample test. Leave-one-state-out refits check each state against the others' predictive interval.
- **Alternatives considered:** `baggr` (Meager's package) or `brms`/Stan: need a Stan toolchain and R package management for a model whose posterior is available exactly; `policy_type` as the covariate (CA and NY are `lea_requirement`, PA's `completion_requirement` would have no pooled counterpart).
- **Reason:** One tau-dimension makes exact grid sampling fast (~10 s) and reproducible with only base R.
- **Result (main prior, no covariate):** FAFSA completion (11 states) mu 0.166 [0.105, 0.229], tau 0.089 [0.047, 0.160]; college enrollment (8 states) mu 0.044 [0.012, 0.076], tau 0.030 [0.003, 0.074]. Results barely move across the three tau priors. In the covariate model, no-diploma states (CA, NY) have a FAFSA effect 0.093 lower (95% [−0.235, 0.050]); for enrollment gamma rests on CA alone and is uninformative. Leave-one-out: Louisiana's first stage (0.377) and Illinois's reduced form (−0.013) fall outside the others' 95% predictive interval.
- **Caveats:** the enrollment outcome differs across states (source-notes), so tau mixes true heterogeneity with definition differences. Pooled estimates inherit the open pre-trend problem (DECISIONS 2026-11-11).
- **Affects:** new `code/12_aggregation.R`, `run.sh`, outputs `pooling_hyper.csv`, `pooling_states.csv`, `pooling_loo.csv`, `pooling_forest.png`; codebook; `tests/test_pipeline.py` (`TestAggregation`).
- **Related issue:**

## 2026-11-11: Test whether graduate counts respond to the mandate

- **Decision:** (user request) `08_reduced_form.py` (`graduate_ratio_test`) runs the main specification with y = graduates ÷ CCD grade-12 enrollment on dose × post in the 8 states with graduate counts (LA, AL, TX, CA, IN, NH, NE, NJ), with and without schools whose ratio ever leaves 0.5–1.5 (`main_trimmed`; drops 0–30 schools a state). Written to `graduate_ratio_dose_test.csv`. An event-time version is in `code/explore/graduate_ratio_event_time.py` (exploratory, not in `run.sh`).
- **Reason:** A graduation requirement could change who graduates. That would make the graduate denominator (and the per-graduate enrollment outcome) respond to the treatment.
- **Result (trimmed):** TX 0.015, CA 0.015, NE −0.015, NJ 0.060 (n.s.); LA 0.102, AL 0.084, IN 0.123, NH 0.129 (p < 0.05). Every coefficient is positive or zero, so graduation does not fall in high-dose schools. The untrimmed NJ estimate (SE 0.46) is driven by one school-year with ratio 53. The positive headline β is partly mechanical. The dose is built from completions ÷ grade 12, so a large grade-12 count in the dose classes raises the dose and lowers the ratio in those same classes. The event-time version (e = −1 omitted, trimmed) shows the same dose-class dip in IN (−0.22, −0.16), NH (−0.31 at e = −3) and CA (−0.08, −0.12). Relative to e = −1, e = 0 is small in IN (+0.03), NH (+0.03), TX (+0.01), NJ (+0.04), CA (−0.05) and NE (−0.06). It is positive in LA (+0.06; e = 1 +0.10, which may reflect LDOE adding Student Transcript System graduates from class 2019) and AL (+0.09, +0.14, with pre coefficients of +0.01 to +0.07).
- **Interpretation:** No evidence that the mandate reduced graduation. If anything, LA and AL graduate counts rose with dose, which would bias the graduate-denominator first stage slightly down. CCD grade 12 stays the baseline denominator.
- **Affects:** `08_reduced_form.py`, new output `graduate_ratio_dose_test.csv`, codebook, `tests/test_pipeline.py` (`test_graduate_ratio_test`).

## 2026-11-02: Event study by state (parallel-trends diagnostic)

- **Decision:** `code/11_event_study.py` replaces D_s × Post with D_s × 1[e = k] for every event time k ≠ −1 (reference class omitted), for FAFSA completion and college enrollment, with the main specification's controls, weights, school and class FE, and school clustering. Sample: the main first-stage schools over all their classes with the outcome and controls (project design: "the event study uses all available years"), so it is unbalanced outside the estimation window; partial-rollout and post-repeal classes (LA after 2024) are left out. No end-point binning. Two joint pre-trend tests: all k ≤ −2, and k ≤ −4 (excluding the dose classes, whose FAFSA coefficients are mechanically negative because dose = 1 − their mean FCR).
- **Alternatives considered:** Balanced panel over all classes (loses schools in long panels; the window is already balanced in the headline); estimation-window classes only (too few pre classes to test trends); binning distant event times (not needed: every class has most schools).
- **Result (first run, CCD grade-12 denominator):** FAFSA completion pre-coefficients slope down toward the dose classes in nearly every state (e.g. CA 0.33 at e = −8 → 0.14 at e = −4; NY 0.23 at e = −10 → −0.05 at e = −4); `pre_no_dose` rejects at the 1% level in every state with classes to test (largest p: OK 0.006). Corrected 2026-11-08: β_k > 0 at early k falling to β < 0 at e = −3/−2 means high-dose schools' completion *fell* relative to low-dose schools into the dose classes and then recovered by e = −1 (a dip that reverses, i.e. mean reversion), not that they were converging up before the mandate (the original reading had the sign backwards). Enrollment pre-trends are flat in LA, AL, NH, NY and MD (`pre_no_dose`), downward in CA, IL, IN, NE, OK, NJ; TX has only dose classes before e = −1 (its outcome starts with the class of 2019) and they are strongly negative (−0.19, −0.17). Louisiana cannot be tested before the dose classes (FSA starts with the class of 2015 = e = −3; Deneault's window starts in 2014). Open: whether the graduate denominator, dose from more classes, or school-specific trends remove this; Rambachan–Roth bounds.
- **Update (same day, user):** added `spec = grad_denom` (completions ÷ state graduate count, dose recomputed; step 8's graduate-denominator schools), overlaid on the main spec in the figures. Runs in LA, AL, MD, TX, CA, IN, NH, NE, NJ (IL has no counts; NY and OK none after the mandate). The downward FAFSA pre-trend is essentially unchanged (e.g. CA 0.38 at e = −8 → 0.16 at e = −4; AL 0.39 → 0.08; IN 0.44 → −0.05), and `pre_no_dose` still rejects at the 1% level in AL, MD, CA, IN, NE, NJ; only NH passes (p = 0.37, 77 schools). In NE the early pre-coefficients get larger. So denominator noise in CCD grade-12 enrollment is not what drives the pre-trend. Graduate counts also cover fewer classes (TX from e = −3, NJ from e = −5; CA and IN have only e = 0 after the mandate). Enrollment pre-trend tests are similar to main.
- **Update (2026-11-08, exploratory, not yet in the pipeline):** Placebo mandates in CT and PA (no mandate before 2027), main screens and controls, dose = 1 − mean fcr in the fake e = −3, −2: with fake first class 2022 (dose 2019–20) both states show the same pattern with no policy (early β ≈ +0.24 to +0.29 falling to ≈ 0 by the dose classes) and CT shows fake post effects of +0.17 to +0.21; with Louisiana's timing (2018; dose 2015–16) only the dose-class dip appears and post β ≈ −0.03. Taking the dose from 2018–19 instead moves the dip to 2018–19 in every state. So the pre-trend is largely mean reversion created by building the dose from the completion rate itself, amplified when the dose classes are 2020–21 (COVID); the post coefficients of states with COVID-era dose classes likely include some recovery. Louisiana's 0.22/0.29 is well outside the placebo.
